Measure the eccentricity the loiter forecast was guessing at

The claim in this repository — and in the roadmap I wrote — was that a pad's mean
eccentricity is near 0.0005 against an osculating 0.000997. Measured, it is the
other way round. Averaging the eccentricity vector over two revolutions on
Vandenberg's committed orbit gives 1.528e-3 against an osculating 9.97e-4. The
number that was there had never been measured by anything.

`meanEccentricity` is the first-order short-period series for the vector, checked
against that average rather than against a closed form: it returns 1.409e-3, and
cuts the vector's spread eightfold on the committed orbit and by up to 205x across
a sweep from 28.5 to 111 degrees of inclination. A spherical field returns the
osculating value unchanged, so a point-mass comparison is untouched.

It is deliberately not what the planner uses, and that is the finding. Feeding it
in made the lifetime forecast worse — 188.4 h against the flown 206.5, where the
osculating value gives 191.4 — and the same measurement says why: on that orbit
perigee swings from 133.6 km to 164.9 km over one short-period cycle, which is a
factor of four in air density. A decay theory that substitutes one perigee for
the whole cycle cannot be repaired by substituting a better one. That
substitution is what is left of the lifetime error, and it belongs in decay.js.

The gate prints both eccentricities side by side at commitment, because the
comparison is the whole of the evidence for the above.
