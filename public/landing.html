Sol · Terra · Luna

A real-time n-body spaceflight simulation. An SLS Block 1 / Orion stack flies itself from Kennedy LC-39B to a Pacific splashdown in 18.81 simulated days — ascent, translunar injection, lunar capture, departure and a guided re-entry, with nobody at the controls.

Every figure on this page is measured from a run, not estimated — backed by 18 verification scripts that fail if the physics drifts.

02

Correct, not merely plausible

Orbital mechanics derived from vis-viva, Kepler and the restricted three-body problem — then verified against an independent propagation before anything is claimed.

RK4 over a flat state vector

Classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta in SI units with no softening term. Accelerations come straight from Newton's law, evaluated once per pair and applied to both bodies with opposite sign, so momentum conservation is exact to floating point.

Total energy holds to better than one part in 10¹².

Closed-loop cutoffs

Every large burn ends on the orbit it achieved, never on a stopwatch. Capture watches selenocentric eccentricity through its minimum; departure watches characteristic energy reach a target. Whatever the engine actually did is already in the number being tested.

C₃ delivered to six parts in 10⁵.

The render loop allocates nothing

Preallocated scratch vectors at module scope, flat Float64Array state, no .map or destructuring in per-frame paths. Verified by heap delta under --expose-gc rather than asserted.

0.019 bytes per frame across 60,000 frames.

Entry heating, both terms

Sutton-Graves gives the convective flux; at 10.5 km/s that is the smaller half. The Tauber-Sutton correlation adds what the shock layer radiates, and the two peak at different altitudes because they scale differently.

156 convective + 256 radiative = 390 W/cm².

Lifting re-entry

A blunt capsule trims at a fixed angle of attack, so lift magnitude is not a control — only its direction. Rolling about the relative wind is the entire authority the vehicle has, and the autopilot uses it to hold a load rather than avoid one.

12.17 g ballistic → 6.28 g guided.

Halo orbits, corrected

A CR3BP differential corrector finds near-rectilinear halo orbits and walks the family by minimum-norm continuation. Full-period closure is checked independently of the corrector's own half-period residual.

Closes to 2.6 × 10⁻¹³ normalised units.
03

Everything is procedural

Earth, Moon and the Milky Way skybox are synthesised in a Web Worker at load, so there are no asset downloads and nothing to break offline. Real NASA imagery is opt-in.

Volumetric atmosphere

Single-scattering Rayleigh and Mie, raymarched in a shell mesh rather than a fullscreen pass — so the GPU depth-tests it for free. Coefficients are real — Rayleigh (5.8, 13.5, 33.1)×10⁻⁶ m⁻¹, Mie asymmetry g = 0.758 — so the limb is blue and grazing light goes orange for the reason it actually does.

Genuine eclipses

A point light inside the Sun casts a cube shadow map, producing real lunar and solar eclipses. An eclipsed Moon glows copper from light refracted through Earth's atmosphere, injected as additive irradiance the shadow map knows nothing about.

A camera that directs itself

Each mission phase names the shot worth watching it from — a ground camera at the pad, the hull during a burn, the geometry during a coast. It requests rather than commands, so taking the camera keeps it.

04

Run it

No API keys, no accounts, no network calls after the first load.

npm install
npm run dev

Then open the simulation, press Start countdown, and leave it alone. The sequencer flies the whole profile. Press h to hide the panels, [ and ] for time warp, 1–4 to lock the camera onto a body.
