# Periapsis Zero: an honest review, 10 October 2026

What a new player sees today, what is weak, and what to do about it.
Written after the boarding, collision and movement fixes in the same
session.

## What works

- **A clear start.** The landing page has one hook ("Somebody sold the
  Aster's route...") and one button, "Begin your journey". The game's own
  title then has a single button, which plays the opening film the first
  time.
- **The story is set up properly.** The intro film gives the world, the
  Hollow, the pilot and the job. The corridor, the bridge briefing and
  the flight deck put faces to the convoy. The ambush pays off the line
  "nobody knows our route". Waking at Hearth states both goals in so many
  words.
- **Always knowing what to do.** One objective at a time, with a
  distance. Prompts appear when you are standing at the thing to use.
  The prologue cannot be failed.
- **Interiors.** The corridor and bridge read as real places: lit
  consoles, a window onto Earth, haze on desktop.
- **Combat feedback is strong:**
  - hit markers and a kill confirmation
  - shields that flare where they are hit
  - ships that break into their pieces
  - a beat of slow motion on a kill

## What is weak, worst first

1. **Act One is a different game.** The prologue hands over to the older
   three.js game, which has different controls, a different interface and
   different, lower-quality characters. That seam is the biggest
   problem; it will feel like falling out of one game into another.
   *Fix:* port Hearth and Act One to Godot, mission by mission. This is
   multi-session work.
2. **People are passable, not good.**
   - **Bodies and faces:** MakeHuman bodies with procedural animation.
     Faces blink and the mouth moves, but there are no real expressions,
     and the lip movement is rhythm, not speech.
   - **Clothes:** the free wardrobe is everyday clothes. The crew wear a
     suit, overalls and a casual jacket, not uniforms.
   - **Animation:** walk, run, crouch, jump and sit are posed in code. They
     read correctly but lack the weight of motion capture.

   *Fix:* retarget CMU motion-capture clips (free for any use) onto the
   game rig, and model a flight suit and crew uniforms in Blender.
3. **No voices.** Every line is a subtitle. For a game that wants a
   cinematic feel, this is the largest missing sense.
   *Fix:* cast and record, or use licensed text-to-speech voices. Either
   way it needs your decision and possibly money.
4. **Sound is thin.** There is one music track, synthesised effects and
   now an interior hum. There are no engine roars, ambient radio or
   material-specific footsteps.
5. **Flight is short and has no stakes.** Six raiders, one beat each, and
   you cannot die. That is right for a prologue, but Act One must bring
   real danger, more enemy types and set pieces.
6. **The ships are good, but procedural.** The Kestrel holds up. The
   freighters are mostly boxes of containers seen from a distance, and the
   Aster needs a distinctive silhouette, because the whole story is about
   her.
7. **Big downloads.**
   - **Browser:** about 45 MB before the title appears, plus a few seconds
     of loading between scenes.
   - **Desktop:** 150 to 180 MB, unsigned, so macOS and Windows warn
     before opening them.

   *Fix:* split the browser build into a small first download and
   stream the rest; sign the Mac build ($99/year Apple developer
   account).
8. **The rooms are empty of life.** Nobody walks the corridors. The flight
   deck has no crew working. The medical bay has one person.
9. **The landing page's display font** (a distressed stencil) clashes with
   the clean typeface used in the game. Pick one voice.

## Fixed in this pass

- **The Kestrel is solid.** You no longer walk through its wings.
  - It sits higher on its cradle, so you can walk under the wings.
  - It is boarded by a ladder at the cockpit.
  - The walkway rail no longer fences you in.

  Checked with an automated walk test (`-- --walktest hangar_ladder`).
- **Interaction works by proximity** (standing at a thing and facing it),
  not by aiming the camera at it. The camera aim made the ship and Hale
  hard to use.
- **New movements:**
  - crouch on C or Ctrl, with a crouched walk
  - jump on Space, with the camera following softly
  - sit in the captain's chair on E, framed from the front

  Each is a new animation baked on the rig.
- **The title screen.** A first visit shows "Begin your journey", which
  plays the opening. After that it offers "Continue the journey", which
  resumes at the last chapter reached.
- **Ambient sound.** A low interior hum plays in every ship scene.
- **The landing page** was rebuilt around the hook, "Begin your journey"
  and a download for your operating system. The career sidebar is hidden
  until you have a pilot.

## Order of work from here

1. **Act One in Godot,** starting with Hearth: walk the station, take
   Mara's first job, and fly it.
2. **Motion capture** for the player and crew, and a flight suit.
3. **Voices:** your call on recorded or synthetic.
4. **Life in the rooms:** crew going about their work.
5. **A smaller first download.**
